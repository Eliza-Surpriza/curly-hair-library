 Card Sort Report 
 Mary Seeley and Eliza Freestone 
 Methods 
 We recruited 10 participants from among our friends and family and each conducted five card sorts. 
 Each of the card sorts were one on ones. Some were done in-person with physical cards, and some 
 done virtually, over phone call, by dragging boxes on Google Drawing. We instructed the participants 
 to sort the cards into categories that made sense to them and write the labels for each category. They 
 shared thoughts as they sorted, and afterwards, we asked them questions of what confused them or 
 why they chose what labels and categories they created. 
 Trends in the Card Sort and Isolated Incidents 
 Three types of labels that showed up in nearly every sort were some variation of products, tools, and 
 information. This general pattern was what we expected to find, since those were the three types of 
 things we decided to include in our card sort. We were surprised by other findings, such as what 
 sub-categories our participants included, and some cards that split between diﬀerent categories. 
 Many cards had strong groups and were sorted together by most participants. Silk bonnet, satin 
 scrunchies, and silk pillowcase were together in every sort. Similarly, every participant sorted the 
 three shampoos together. The two influencers were always put in the same group, and frequently 
 placed with other informational items. Curl cream and mousse were together in 90% of the sorts, as 
 well as microfiber hair towel and towel bonnet. 
 One theme that may not show up in our standardized labels is how often our participants emphasized 
 the sequential nature of a curly hair routine. Many people sorted into specific times you would use 
 certain products (“wash day”, “curly hair aftercare”, “sleep”). One participant organized her piles step 
 by step, based on what order the cards would show up in a routine. Another participant combined 
 liquid products and physical tools into the same categories, because they would be used in the same 
 steps of the routine. 
 Some participants did broader categories (Product, Tools, and Resources) while others did more 
 specific categories that usually landed within the other participants' larger category (Product -> 
 Shower routine). This leads us to believe that having a tree might be better, with the larger categories 
 as the trunk and smaller categories as the leaves. Some people had diﬀerent branches with 
 information. Some grouped people separately, and others combined them with instructions and tips. 
 The cards that were splitters were Homemade flax-seed hair gel recipe, “Plopping” instructions, and 
 Leave-in conditioner. The recipe had the biggest split, with 50% putting it with products, 30% putting 
 it in information, and 20% putting it in a whole new category (Recipes, Why not try it) because they 
 didn’t feel it belonged. “Plopping” instructions 80% had it with instructions, and 20% had it with 
 “wash day stuﬀ” and “Keep Curls Fresh,” which have more product-related items. The last splitter was 
 leave-in conditioner. Three people said during washing and two said after. 
 Categories and Labels 
 According to the results, it would make most sense to have two organizational schemes: by type 
 (products, tools, education), and a sequential organization of when you would use the product or tool 
 or need the information. 
 Here are the labels and sub-labels for the first organizational scheme, as well as an explanation of why 
 we are including that category. 
 ●  Curly Hair Products: 
 -  10/10  Every sort had some version of products. Some split it into styling and 
 shampoo/conditioner. This is from 6’s label and is topical. 
 ○  Shampoo/Conditioner: 
 -  5/10 separated into washing and 2/10 explicitly said shampoo and conditioner 
 this is from 1’s label and is topical. 
 ○  Styling Products: 
 -  4/10 separated based on styling products, taken from 4’s label and is topical 
 ●  Tools: 
 -  9/10 Most sorted tools either together or between the following two groups. This label 
 was taken from 3, 9, 10 all used “tools”. These are topical 
 ○  Styling: 4/10 
 -  Physical objects to help define and refresh curls, label taken from 5, 9,10 
 ○  Protecting 
 -  5/10 Objects to put hair in for sleep or after shower to keep the curls looking 
 nice. Label from 5, 10 
 ●  Education 
 -  10/10 Everyone put all of the education materials together. Some broke it down into 
 smaller categories. Label taken from 5, 8. These are topical 
 ○  Instructions 
 -  2/10 “How to” information. The two labels given were “to-do” (1) and “advise” 
 (2) we combined them into instructions 
 ○  Science 
 -  4/10 Information on how the hair works, we used the label from 1 ,2, 9 who all 
 used “science” 
 ○  Influencers 
 -  7/10 sorted them separately. These are social media people who post on curly 
 hair. 
 Here are the labels for the sequential organization: 
 ●  Washing 
 -  5/10 specifically stated wash day. Unlike the one above, this is sequential and includes 
 not only products (liquid and plastic), but also educational material as well, including 
 videos from influencers. Washing label from 3, 4 
 ●  Drying 
 -  2/10 same with washing diﬀerent kinds of items related to drying, label from 4 
 ●  Styling 
 -  3/10 same as before with diﬀerent items related to styling, label from 4, 5 
 ●  Sleeping 
 -  3/10 same as before but related to sleeping, label from 4, 10 
 ●  Touch up 
 -  2/10 same as before but related to how to keep your hair looking nice a few days after 
 wash day. Label is from 1. 
 What We’ll Ignore 
 One thing we’ll ignore is that most people who labeled our two influencer cards into their own group 
 called it “people,” and no one labeled it “influencers.” We felt that “influencers” was a more clear label, 
 and that our participants may not have used it because our cards said influencers on them. 
 For now, we’re ignoring the input from Participant 4 to put recipes and hair cuts as their own 
 categories. If we add more information on hair cuts or more recipes, we may make those a 
 subcategory under education, but for now, they can go under Education -> Instructions since they each 
 only have one item. 
 We’ll also ignore how Participant 7 put the flaxseed hair gel in a “Shower routine” category, since it is 
 actually a product you would use after the shower, not during.
